## Findings

1. **The invented annual account is correct.** Normal resources are 90 + 14 = 104 and costs are 60 + 10 + 25 + 5 = 100, leaving 4. Adverse resources are 80 + 10 = 90 and costs are 60 + 12 + 26 + 5 = 103, leaving a deficit of 13. A starting reserve of 26 pays two such annual deficits and then reaches zero. Restoration needs 26/4 = 6.5 annual-surplus equivalents. Six year-end deposits provide only 24; the seventh provides 28. The final draft states the year-end timing limit and denies that annual totals establish cash availability at each payment date. The example also excludes new investment and principal repayment, and assumes that other public duties have separate funding. These limits prevent it from being mistaken for a national funding estimate.

2. **Stocks and flows remain distinct.** Sections 4–5 distinguish an asset receipt, cash income, sale proceeds and retained capital. A transfer of an existing public asset does not create wealth. Borrowing is financing, with interest and principal obligations; it is not recurring tax income. Acquisition and construction have their own entries, and later operating costs remain in the recurring account. The reserve cannot also pay for the same construction project. Internal transfers are removed in consolidated cash accounts. This is consistent with S005 and S008, including their limits on finite reserves and expected equity returns.

3. **Revenue is stated on a net and conditional basis.** The draft requires tax interactions, collection costs, displaced income, fees and other claims to be visible, with collection costs counted once. It rejects adding pre-tax profit to a full dividend and avoids reusing receipts already committed elsewhere. The import-tax account retains possible domestic losses (S006). The equity-levy discussion retains S015's weighting conditions and does not treat shares as immediate cash. The new weak-profit case is useful: high AI output does not supply an assumed large profit-tax base. The consumption-tax comparison explicitly keeps S012's fixed-income and common-saving limits and does not rank that tax as best.

4. **The transition order is coherent.** Stages overlap. Revenue work and slow supply projects start early; emergency support does not wait for an investment fund or an international agreement. Existing lawful routes continue while replacements are tested. Construction needs current finance before it adds supply, and service operation is separately budgeted. The final stage-5 text applies the joint review to discretionary increases, while emergency relief and already-authorized adjustments continue under their own rules. The dates are planning proposals, not results established by the models. An actual implementation still needs the stated country-specific appropriations, collection estimates and cash schedule.

5. **The payment promise matches the fiscal claim.** Section 6 separates a protected minimum from a variable addition, identifies the cost of price adjustment and avoids calling a fixed nominal payment constant purchasing power. It places current duties, reserve restoration where required, and upkeep before a larger variable dividend. Continued financial or physical insufficiency requires a public decision; it is not concealed by a payment delay. The comparison rule follows S018 without treating its simulations as success probabilities. International promises remain supplements with a failure plan, consistent with S009's conditional payment and deposit results.
