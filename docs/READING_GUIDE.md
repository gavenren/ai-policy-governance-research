# Governing an economy when wages no longer support most people
## A guide to the research papers

Prepared on 12 October 2026. This guide connects studies S004–S015. It is an explanation of their results, not a new study or a combined economic model. The papers are AI-assisted complete-study drafts with separate AI reviews. They have not had human or journal peer review.

## Start with the promise to the household

The programme asks a conditional question: suppose AI can perform economically useful work so cheaply that competitive wages no longer provide a dependable income. How could people still obtain what they need, and retain a say in the institutions on which they depend? None of these papers predicts when that condition will occur. None assumes that the loss of paid work makes human activity less valuable.

A useful starting point is a household promise, rather than a preferred tax or investment fund. A promise to pay a fixed amount is different from a promise to secure food, housing and care. A promise to maintain consumption is different again from a promise to service all existing debts. The choice affects both the resources required and who benefits.

[A Consumption Floor After Human Labor (S004)](../studies/2026-10-11-post-labor-floor/paper.md) makes this distinction through a small model. Goods that can be reproduced cheaply do not remove scarcity in an essential service. In its illustrative base case, recipients can buy more than the stated floor for reproducible goods but less than the essential-service floor. Expanding supply can help, although building it uses resources that could otherwise support current payments. The result is a reason to specify real access and transition costs together. It is not an estimate of the tax share that an actual country needs.

[Allocating a Universal Compute Floor (S014)](../studies/2026-10-12-compute-allocation/paper.md) asks what a promised share of computing resources means when requests differ. A formula can distribute the full budget and still give someone more than their stated demand. The paper checks one proposal, keeps the properties that hold, and compares it with an established capped allocation rule. It also shows why a rule must say how an earlier allocation changes when the available budget changes. A mathematical priority weight alone does not settle the meaning of a public entitlement.

[What Remains After the Payment? (S011)](../studies/2026-10-11-household-debt/paper.md) examines another part of the household promise. It uses public U.S. survey data and removes recorded wages while holding debt payments fixed. In its central example, a monthly transfer of $2,000 per family puts every family above a $1,000 resource threshold before payments. About 22.9 percent fall below the same threshold after the survey's constructed debt-service amount. This is a conditional accounting result, not a forecast of default or hardship. In particular, the fixed amount is per family; it does not establish equal support for families with different needs.

The [equal-budget extension to S011](../studies/2026-10-11-household-debt/paper.md#8-extension-equal-budgets-can-produce-different-answers) then changes the payment unit. It compares equal payments per family, per adult and in proportion to the square root of family size. Total gross funding and the needs rule are held fixed. A rule that leaves fewer families below the threshold can still leave more people living in families below it. This is a reason to report both counts and the amount of unmet need. It is not evidence that one payment unit is best for every policy objective.

The debt finding does not show that public funds should pay every creditor. It shows that the proposal must state which claim it protects. Debt relief, changed payment contracts and a larger transfer distribute losses differently. The paper holds those responses outside its main calculation so the underlying choice remains visible.

## Establish where the resources come from

After defining the promise, the next task is to identify resources that can support it. Four papers examine different routes. They do not establish a single preferred source of revenue.

[Buying a Public Stake Without Buying a Guarantee (S008)](../studies/2026-10-11-public-capital-acquisition/paper.md) starts before the state owns the asset. A public purchase needs a seller, a price and a source of finance. Borrowing to buy a risky asset can produce a favorable expected return and still leave the state with a large loss when households need support. Acquisition can also improve protection if its returns offset weak tax receipts. In that case, its value for the guarantee comes from the pattern of public resources across states. A fair-price purchase has not created free financial wealth.

[Funding a Post-Labor Income Floor When AI Is Imported (S006)](../studies/2026-10-11-automation-imports/paper.md) studies a country that buys AI from abroad. A levy can generate receipts while increasing domestic production costs and reducing other public revenue. A government must therefore distinguish gross tax receipts, the net change in its transfer budget, and the change in national consumption. The model can produce an increase in transfer funding alongside a fall in domestic consumption. This does not settle the ethical choice: a society may accept an aggregate cost to help people with greater needs. It does prevent the cost from disappearing from the accounts.

[How Much Would an AI Equity Levy Collect? (S015)](../studies/2026-10-12-equity-levy-aggregation/paper.md) examines a proposed levy tied to sales into participating markets. A cap applied to each firm can make group averages insufficient to determine collection. The paper constructs the receipt ranges compatible with the same group inputs. When the group sales means use firm equity values as weights, full participation removes this particular source of uncertainty, including uncertainty about each member’s share. The analysis does not validate the input values or establish that firms would keep their value and behavior after a levy.

[Who Can Rely on an International AI Dividend? (S009)](../studies/2026-10-11-international-dividend/paper.md) considers a recurring agreement between a provider country and a recipient country. The question is whether the provider will still pay after it has received the current benefit from cooperation. Average gains are not enough. Persistent adverse states can weaken the future gain that supports payment. A financed deposit can help under the model's enforcement assumptions, but its cost can also undermine participation. The paper does not establish that any existing treaty meets these conditions.

## Match the promise to the payment date

A fund can own enough assets in total and still lack cash on the day a payment is due. [Cash Before Capital (S005)](../studies/2026-10-11-dividend-reserves/paper.md) tests that timing problem. The required reserve is determined by the largest cumulative cash shortfall within the allowed revenue paths. A sale that settles too late cannot cover an earlier deadline. Selling an income-producing asset can also reduce future receipts. The result concerns liquidity within specified paths; the fraction of synthetic paths passed is not a real-world probability of success.

Even cash ready for payment does not establish that an eligible person receives it. [When a Funded Benefit Still Does Not Arrive (S007)](../studies/2026-10-11-benefit-access/paper.md) compares review queues and advance-payment rules. It separates money already owed to eligible people from additional money needed for advances to people later found ineligible. A stable review queue can coexist with recurring unrecovered losses. The public budget must fund those losses as well as the entitlement. Its favorable comparison for payment at the deadline assumes that payment at that time works reliably.

[Buying Backup AI for Essential Public Services (S013)](../studies/2026-10-12-supplier-continuity/paper.md) asks what happens when delivery depends on an AI service. Two different sellers need not provide two independent ways to complete a task. Named models, platforms, access rules and support dates can eliminate a proposed alternative. The four-record case audit identifies candidate pairs under explicit exclusions, but all of its operational-readiness conditions remain unknown. Its central practical distinction is between an alternative listed in a plan and an alternative demonstrated to work for the required service.

## Give people a route to the right decision-maker

Financial backing and administrative delivery leave another question: who can change the promise, and who can correct a failure?

[Who Governs the People's Capital? (S010)](../studies/2026-10-11-public-ownership-control/paper.md) follows payment, investment-voting and review authority in three public funds. The institutions have different purposes. They are not three interchangeable basic-income systems. The comparison shows why a payment appeal, a government budget decision and a corporate ownership vote need different routes for review. An investment manager cannot necessarily restore a benefit cut by another body. A benefit appeal cannot necessarily reverse a shareholder vote.

The paper argues that an AI fund proposal should name the body responsible for each promise and the body able to correct each type of failure. It does not establish that its proposed arrangement prevents political capture or performs better than another fund. Those are separate questions.

## Use measures that match the claim

A technical comment makes the measurement question concrete. [Measuring the AI Dividend (S012)](../studies/2026-10-12-redistribution-audit/paper.md) checks four claims in one version of an AI redistribution preprint. It reports both corrections and results that survive scrutiny.

Its simplest example needs no advanced mathematics. Incomes of 1 and 3 become 2 and 4 after each person receives 1. The gap remains 2, while the ratio falls from three to two. An unchanged absolute gap therefore does not imply unchanged relative inequality. The paper also distinguishes an exact production identity from a local approximation, and identifies the assumptions under which two other results hold. These checks do not prove that a transfer is affordable or that one tax is best. They make the claims precise enough to assess.

## How to use the papers together

A reader can follow the household promise through four decisions: define the protected outcome; establish public resources; deliver support on time; and assign authority for correction. The papers isolate parts of this sequence. Their assumptions differ, so their numerical examples cannot be added together or treated as a jointly validated policy package.

For a short reading path, begin with S004 on real access, S011 on household obligations, and S010 on public authority. Then follow the financing or delivery question relevant to the proposal. Each study folder contains its methods, source records, results, limitations and AI review. The [study queue](STUDY_QUEUE.md) records the bounded contribution of each paper.

The remaining work is substantial. The programme has not solved the joint response of prices, investment, international cooperation, household contracts and political institutions to permanent labor displacement. Its present contribution is a set of explicit, inspectable distinctions that a more complete policy analysis must preserve.
