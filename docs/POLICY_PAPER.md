# A Secure Living After Wages
## What governments should do, in what order, and how to pay for it

**Policy paper · 12 October 2026**  
**Basis:** A synthesis of the 15 economic-governance papers, S004–S018, in this repository.  
**Status:** AI-assisted policy draft. This paper proposes a course of action; it does not report a new combined economic model. No human or journal peer review has occurred.

### Abstract

Suppose AI can perform economically useful work so cheaply that wages no longer support most people. Governments would then need a way to distribute income that does not depend on employment. This paper proposes a basic income right, essential public services, and a public claim on economic returns. It draws together 15 studies of real access, public finance, household debt, payment delivery, ownership, and policy measurement. Their common lesson is that a payment promise must pass several separate tests. Resources must exist. Government must be able to collect a share. Cash must be ready when due. People must receive it and obtain essential goods. Public bodies must have clear duties and powers to correct failures. The proposed sequence starts with payment access and essential supply before severe income loss. It then provides emergency support when household income fails, while permanent revenue replaces dependence on wage taxes. Taxes form the initial funding base. Public investment returns can add support after acquisition costs and risks are counted. Reserves and borrowing cover defined temporary gaps, not an unexplained permanent deficit. The paper specifies an annual funding account, a separate cash account, and conditions for increasing payments. These are policy recommendations informed by the studies. They are not a forecast, a national cost estimate, or a proved optimal programme.

### 1. The problem and the decision

An economy can produce more while leaving many people without enough income to buy what it produces. This becomes a central problem if owners receive most of the returns from AI and workers can no longer earn a dependable living. Higher output alone does not give every household a claim on it.

This paper takes that condition as a planning assumption. It does not claim that it has arrived or give a date for its arrival. Being better at tasks also does not, by itself, prove that all paid human activity will end. The policy question here is narrower: what should government do if wages cease to provide adequate income for most people?

The proposed answer is to make basic living conditions independent of paid work. People should receive dependable income and have access to food, housing, energy, health care, and other essentials. They should retain a voice in the institutions that provide this support. Education, care, art, science, and public life can remain valuable even where markets pay little for them. Under the assumed condition, training alone cannot restore wages as the main means of support.

The 15 studies identify failures that this system must avoid. They use different methods and assumptions. Some use small models. One uses household survey data. Others check public institutions or specific proposals. Their numerical results cannot be added to form a national budget. The contribution of this paper is to connect their findings to decisions, responsible bodies, and funding accounts. The order of action is a proposal, not a result proved by the studies.

### 2. Define the public promise before choosing the fund

The government should establish three linked forms of support.

First, provide a regular cash payment that does not require employment. The recommended default is an individual payment for eligible adults, with a separate child payment. The tax system can recover support from people with high incomes. This reduces the need to stop a basic payment while an income claim is under review. It also creates a large gross payment budget, so the government must publish both gross cost and cost after tax recovery. A well-run income-tested guarantee is another option. The papers do not establish which option has the lowest total cost or error rate.

Second, provide additional support for needs that a uniform payment cannot meet. These include disability, care needs, and household circumstances. Parliament must define residence rules, child coverage, access for people without standard documents, and treatment of people who share costs. It should compare outcomes per person as well as per household. The household-debt study shows why these choices can change which payment rule appears best. [S011]

Third, secure access to essential services. Some support can take the form of public provision, insurance, or purchases from regulated providers. Cash should cover costs that households must still pay. The budget must avoid paying twice for the same need. For example, a defined health service can be available without a charge. Government can then exclude that charge when it calculates the cash payment. It must still budget the service's full public cost.

The minimum should describe real access, not only a currency amount. A larger payment can fail if rents rise or care places remain unavailable. Expanding supply can help, but construction uses resources before the new service becomes available. Government must therefore fund present needs and the transition to greater supply together. [S004]

A proposed payment law should state the amount, payment date, eligibility rules, adjustment method, and appeal route. It should also name the body that must pay. A public investment fund is not itself a personal right to income. [S010]

### 3. Put the actions in this order

The stages below overlap. Revenue reform and slow construction projects must start early. Emergency support must not wait for a new public fund to grow. The dates are proposed planning targets after a government adopts this programme. They are not forecasts of AI progress or evidence that every country can meet them.

| Stage | Main action and responsible body | Required result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Prepare now; first 90 days** | The cabinet assigns a lead ministry. The finance ministry costs existing obligations. Payment and service agencies identify people at risk of exclusion. Local authorities identify essential shortages. | A public statement of the protected living standard, a gap report, named duties, and a funded emergency route. |
| **2. Establish access; start at once and test within the first year** | Payment agencies test enrolment, deadlines, appeals, and backups. Parliament resolves benefit conflicts. Service agencies start urgent repairs and projects with long lead times. | People can receive support through a tested route. Existing services continue during the change. |
| **3. Respond whenever household income fails** | The government uses existing lawful support and emergency powers where available. Parliament supplies any additional authority and funding. | Temporary support begins on the basis of need, without requiring each person to prove that AI caused the loss. |
| **4. Establish permanent funding; work begins in stage 1** | The finance ministry and tax authority reform collection. Parliament sets the payment and funding rules. A public fund is considered only with a costed acquisition plan. | A recurring budget, a cash reserve plan, and stress tests support the permanent promise. |
| **5. Review before discretionary increases** | Parliament reviews finance, access, delivery, and public oversight at least annually. | Any discretionary increase has financial backing and a plan for essential supply. Emergency relief and already-authorized payment adjustments continue under their own rules. |

#### 3.1 Prepare without waiting for a final diagnosis

The first report should track household resources, job access, wage income, essential prices, tax receipts, and payment failures. These measures should cover people who have left the labour force as well as registered unemployed people. The report should distinguish income earned in markets from support already paid by government.

Relief should respond to observed hardship. A household does not need a final economic explanation before it needs food or housing. A broad permanent reform requires a wider review of lasting income loss, future revenue, and public choices. Using these two decisions separately permits fast relief without treating every temporary fall in employment as proof of permanent displacement.

#### 3.2 Keep services and payments working during the change

Use payment routes that already work while testing replacements. Provide access for people who cannot use a digital account. Set a maximum review period and a route for urgent cases. Where temporary advance payments protect basic needs, budget for the share that will not be recovered. Money already owed to an eligible person is an unpaid obligation, not a new benefit to be counted a second time. [S007]

AI can help administer support, but a second supplier name is not proof of a working backup. The agency must test the complete task, including data access, staff actions, and payment completion. A non-AI fallback may be appropriate for some essential tasks. The supplier study did not establish the readiness of its candidate backups. [S013]

#### 3.3 Begin essential investment before shortages become severe

Local authorities should identify where more housing, energy capacity, care, transport, or other services would remove a binding shortage. Projects need operating budgets as well as construction budgets. Where new supply will take years, government must state how it will allocate existing access during that period. Such rules should be public and open to appeal.

A computing entitlement can support access to AI, but it does not replace food, housing, or income. If government supplies computing resources, it should set demand limits, priorities, and rules for unused capacity. It should state how allocations change when the budget changes. The compute study shows that these properties cannot be assumed from a formula's stated purpose. [S014]

### 4. Fund permanent support from recurring public resources

The initial funding base should be the general public budget, with a planned shift away from dependence on wage taxes. Relevant candidates include corporate profits and income from investments. Stronger collection and removal of poorly justified reliefs may also help. Each change needs a revenue estimate that includes its effects on investment, prices, and other taxes. This direction is consistent with the IMF's 2024 case for stronger capital-income taxation and wider social protection. The IMF work does not establish the rates or payment amounts proposed here. [B1]

The government should choose the mix after a country-specific review. A tax with a large stated base may collect little if enforcement is weak. A levy paid by a company may ultimately reduce household income or raise prices. The objective is a dependable public share of economic resources, with the distribution of gains and costs made clear.

Large AI output also need not produce large taxable profits. Competition may reduce profits, while tax avoidance or foreign ownership may limit domestic receipts. A costed plan must test this case. It should compare a broader tax base, including consumption with equal rebates, where profit-based revenue proves weak. S012 supports the inequality effect of one rebate arrangement only under fixed-income and common-saving assumptions. It does not establish that such a tax is best. The government must show the payment and the tax together. [S012, S016]

| Source or instrument | Role in the proposed plan | What the budget must show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recurring taxes** | Main initial source for continuing payments and services. | Net additional receipts after tax interactions, reliefs, and likely responses. Collection costs must appear once in the accounts. |
| **Reallocation of existing spending** | A possible contribution where an old programme is genuinely replaced. | The obligation that ends, who loses support, and whether new costs replace the claimed saving. |
| **Public investment returns** | A later source of income and a way to spread ownership. | Purchase cost, fees, losses, debt costs, required reinvestment, and cash available for public spending. |
| **International revenue sharing** | A possible supplement, especially for countries with little AI ownership. | Payment duties, enforcement, verification, timing, and a plan for default. |
| **Cash reserves** | Cover temporary gaps between receipts and payments. | How the reserve was funded, when it can be used, and how it will be restored. |
| **Borrowing or asset sales** | Finance approved investment or a defined temporary gap. | Interest, repayment dates, sale delays, lower future asset income, and the source of later funding. |

#### 4.1 Use public ownership with an explicit purchase plan

A public fund can receive assets bought with past surpluses, assets already owned by the state, or new assets acquired under a lawful policy. Each route has a cost or an existing claim attached to it. Moving an asset between public bodies does not increase public wealth. An equity levy can transfer shares to the state. These shares are an asset receipt. Their value is not also cash available for this year's payment.

Borrowing to buy shares can produce a positive expected return and still weaken support during a joint fall in taxes and asset income. The fund should report these joint risks. Its spending rule should distinguish cash income, asset sales, and retained capital. It should preserve enough capital to support its stated future role. A diversified fund may help, but the acquisition study does not prove that a particular portfolio will secure the payment. [S008]

Do not count a company's pre-tax profit as both tax revenue and a full public dividend. The dividend estimate must use profit after the relevant taxes and other claims. Do not treat an increase in share prices as a recurring cash receipt. When shares are sold, record both the cash and the loss of the future ownership claim.

#### 4.2 Test taxes on imported AI and proposed equity levies

A country that imports AI should estimate the domestic price response before treating an import charge as foreign-funded support. The charge can increase receipts while reducing domestic output or other revenue. More money for transfers can still be a defensible distributional choice, but the cost must remain visible. [S006]

An equity levy with a company-level cap needs company-level information, or a range that reflects missing information. Industry averages can conceal material differences in receipts. The bounds in S015 depend on its specified weighting assumptions; they are not a ready national revenue forecast. [S015]

#### 4.3 Treat international promises according to their strength

Countries with little AI ownership may need international cooperation as well as domestic reform. A sharing agreement should state what is paid, when it is paid, and what happens if a member leaves. Payment incentives can weaken during prolonged adverse conditions. A funded deposit can help under some conditions, but its financing cost also matters. [S009]

A government should not include a discretionary foreign promise in the funding for an unconditional basic payment. Even a stronger agreement needs a domestic continuity plan. Starting international talks early is useful; delaying immediate household support until those talks succeed is not.

### 5. Keep annual funding, investment, and cash timing separate

The finance ministry should publish three linked accounts. Each should reconcile with the whole public budget. It must not assign tax receipts to this programme while leaving the same receipts committed elsewhere.

The **recurring account** compares continuing costs with continuing resources. Costs include the basic payment, additional needs support, service operation, administration, expected unrecovered advances, and interest. Resources include collected taxes and spendable public investment income. Confirmed savings from replaced programmes should reduce costs or increase available budget space once, not both.

The **investment account** records construction, public asset purchases, and their financing. A project needs both its initial cost and later running costs. Debt principal repayments belong in the financing schedule even though they are not a new operating expense. Borrowing creates cash now and a repayment obligation; it is not tax income.

The **cash account** starts with available cash, adds receipts that actually settle, and subtracts payments when due. It includes borrowing, principal repayments, asset purchases and sales, and reserve transfers where relevant. Transfers between public accounts do not create new resources for the government as a whole. Report consolidated cash after removing such internal transfers.

The annual recurring gap is the continuing cost minus reliable continuing resources. A positive gap requires additional resources or an explicit revision to the programme. A reserve can cover a temporary gap while those measures take effect. It cannot remove a persistent gap. [S005]

#### An example of the distinction

The following numbers are invented accounting units. They illustrate timing; they are not a tax target, a benefit proposal, or an estimate for any country. Assume that all other public obligations already have separate funding. Assume no new investment, asset purchases, or debt principal payments in this small example.

| Annual item | Normal year | Adverse year |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Net tax resources available to this programme | 90 | 80 |
| Spendable public investment income | 14 | 10 |
| **Total recurring resources** | **104** | **90** |
| Basic cash payments | 60 | 60 |
| Additional needs support | 10 | 12 |
| Essential-service operation | 25 | 26 |
| Administration, advance losses, and interest | 5 | 5 |
| **Total recurring cost** | **100** | **103** |
| **Annual balance** | **+4** | **−13** |

A reserve of 26 units, funded before this example starts, can cover two complete adverse years on these assumptions. It will then be empty. Restoring it requires 6.5 times the normal annual surplus of 4. With deposits only at year-end, restoration requires seven complete normal years, assuming no other use or shock. This is annual arithmetic. It does not prove that cash is available at every payment date within each year.

If adverse conditions persist, government needs a further decision before the reserve runs out. If it also proposes a construction project, that project needs a separate funding entry. The same 26 units cannot simultaneously secure the payment reserve and pay for the project. The budget must also explain where the original reserve came from.

Stress tests should combine low tax receipts, weak asset returns, rising essential costs, and payment delays. They should test prolonged conditions as well as single bad years. Report the remaining cash and the size of unmet need for each case. A fraction of model cases passed is not an estimated probability that the real programme will succeed. [S005, S008]

### 6. Protect a minimum before paying a variable dividend

The recommended design separates a protected basic payment from an additional dividend that can vary with resources. Government should not make food or housing depend directly on this year's fund return. Parliament should approve a rule for adjusting the basic payment to the costs households must meet. The cash rule must take account of services supplied directly, so the same cost is not counted twice.

Price adjustment is a choice, not free insurance. It raises public costs when prices rise. If essential supply cannot respond, more cash alone cannot fulfil the access promise. The budget should show the cost of the adjustment and the measures that address the shortage. A fixed nominal payment should never be described as a guarantee of unchanged buying power. [S004, S018]

Before paying a larger variable dividend, government should meet current obligations, restore the approved reserve where required, and fund necessary service upkeep. The size of any further increase should follow a published rule. This order is a recommended protection for basic needs; the studies do not estimate its optimal parameters.

If a lasting funding gap appears, the finance ministry should publish its cause and present options to parliament. These can include additional revenue, changes to spending outside the protected minimum, and a pause in discretionary increases. Temporary borrowing requires repayment capacity. If feasible resources still cannot meet the minimum, parliament must disclose the unmet obligation and adopt emergency priorities. No law or reserve can guarantee goods that the economy cannot supply. A silent payment delay should not substitute for that public decision.

Every budget comparison should hold the payment rule constant or identify the change. A smaller deficit obtained by fixing payments instead of allowing increases reflects a different promise. It is not, by itself, evidence of a better policy. [S018]

### 7. Resolve debts, benefit rules, and public authority

Income support must address the household position after payment. Existing loans may depend on wages that no longer arrive. Yet paying every old contract in full could direct much of the programme to creditors. Government should examine payment extensions, negotiated losses, and other lawful debt-resolution options. It must report effects on borrowers, creditors, financial institutions, and public budgets. The choice should protect basic needs without disguising a general creditor guarantee as income support. [S011]

Benefit rules need a separate review. A provision that excludes new cash from one income test can have a different effect under another test. S017 identifies such an issue in one proposal and one captured health-coverage rule. It does not establish actual coverage losses or a general power to change the law. The recommendation here is to check the full household result before changing benefits, secure the required authority, and prevent gaps during the transition. [S017]

Parliament should assign separate duties for payment, investment, service provision, and review. A payment appeal must reach a body that can order a payment correction. An investment complaint needs a body with authority over the investment decision. Publish fund mandates, fees, voting policies, conflicts of interest, and audited accounts. These are proposed safeguards, not proof that public ownership will prevent capture. [S010]

Local service duties also need funding from the level of government that assigns them. A national income promise should not leave a local authority with an unfunded service obligation. The national accounts should include these transfers once and show whether the receiving body has the capacity to provide the service.

### 8. Measure whether people can live securely

The government should publish a short quarterly report with an annual independent audit. This reporting schedule is a policy proposal. It should include:

- Access to food, housing, energy, and essential care, including waiting times and unmet need.
- Resources remaining after essential costs, with debt payments shown separately.
- Payment coverage, late payments, appeal times, and corrections.
- Net recurring revenue, recurring cost, cash reserves, debt payments, and investment commitments.
- Outcomes by household size, income group, and additional needs, with personal data protected.
- Both absolute income gaps and relative inequality, with the measure defined.

These measures answer different questions. Giving everyone the same amount can reduce relative inequality while leaving the currency gap unchanged. Neither result alone establishes that needs are met. [S012] Rapid measured AI output growth does not establish spendable government revenue. [S016] A lower annual deficit does not establish that a payment is sufficient or that a previous debt has been repaid. [S018]

Before a major expansion, an independent review should test the same payment promise against several economic paths. It should identify assumptions about prices, investment, imports, tax collection, household debt, and public capacity. A published disagreement about those assumptions is more useful than a single precise result with no stated limits.

### 9. What this proposal can and cannot establish

The studies support a disciplined way to assess a programme. They do not show that a particular universal payment, tax rate, fund size, or reserve will work everywhere. Their models do not jointly resolve changes in prices, investment, international cooperation, debt contracts, and political power. The later audits often correct one proposal under narrow conditions. Those corrections must not become claims about every possible policy.

The next step for an actual government is a public, country-specific budget and access assessment. It should compare a universal payment with an income-tested guarantee, include essential services and transition investment, and show who pays and who gains. Delivery trials can test enrolment and appeals. They cannot establish the full economic effects of permanent mass displacement. Human review is needed from public-finance researchers, service agencies, legal specialists, and people likely to depend on the programme.

Establish access and emergency support early. Build continuing public revenue and expand essential supply at the same time. Increase the permanent promise as evidence permits. At each stage, the government must answer five questions: What is promised? Who must provide it? What resources support it? When must it arrive? Who can correct a failure?
