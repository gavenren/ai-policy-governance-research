## Contribution and selection

The contribution is narrow and credible on the terms used in the manuscript. It applies known distinctions among owners, managers, spending bodies, and review bodies to an AI income proposal. It is not a new theory of accountability. The paper states this limit clearly.

The reviewer checked the role categories and comparison cautions in Truman's chapter; the owner, governing-body, management, and accountability provisions in the Santiago Principles; Bruenig's voting alternatives; and sections 2 and 4 of Stout and Gramitto. The last source does combine income rights with corporate voice and states that income is variable. It also presents payment conditional on proxy selection as a possible participation incentive. The manuscript represents these points correctly. Its proposal to protect a necessary income floor from such a participation condition is a conditional policy argument, not an observed causal result.

The three funds are selected for different purposes and cannot rank governance systems or estimate population frequencies. This is not fatal to the stated descriptive question. The manuscript does not treat a pension reserve, a state budget fund, and a resident dividend as equivalent income rights. It also discloses the English-language search, missing early search times, unavailable recent article, and lack of priority proof. A stronger novelty or completeness claim would not pass this review.
