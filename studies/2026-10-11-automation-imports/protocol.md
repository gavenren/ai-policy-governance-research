## Question and contribution

When can a levy on imported AI increase transfers to people without assets, while it preserves aggregate domestic consumption? The intended contribution is a closed-form joint feasibility limit, including displaced domestic revenue, collection costs, and one common policy across adverse states. Tax incidence, foreign-rent extraction, and the gap between revenue and welfare maxima are established ideas. The paper will not claim them as new.

## Scope and method

The unit of analysis is a synthetic policy-state pair. The model is a static open economy with no wage income. Domestic AI users face a decreasing marginal product; foreign suppliers have an increasing supply price. Public revenue includes a fixed share of domestic rents and a share of actual levy payments. The latter share measures collection/resource losses, not unpaid tax. Transfers return to domestic consumers. All other income belongs to domestic asset owners. No country forecast or causal estimate is planned.

The primary outcome is the largest increase in transfer funding consistent with no fall in total domestic consumption. Secondary outcomes are gross levy receipts, lost domestic tax revenue, recipient income, owner income, imports, and foreign producer income. A positive transfer result with a negative aggregate result is a required counterexample.

Input choices will cover full and partial pass-through, low collection efficiency, a higher public share of domestic rents, positive fixed administration costs, low baseline AI demand, and a foreign withdrawal limit. No probabilities attach to these states. All inputs are synthetic and complete; no empirical missingness is implied.

## Changes

- During source reading, found Becker (2026). Narrowed contribution from general incidence to a joint floor and no-loss feasibility test.
- Found Richter (2021) and Pujolas and Rossbach (2026). Removed any claim that import-country incentives, fiscal maxima, or cooperative revenue sharing are new.
- Distinguished an efficiency loss in collecting an actual wedge from evasion that eliminates the wedge. These require different models.
- Added a true small-country limit: flat foreign supply gives full pass-through and no positive welfare-safe levy in this model.

All discussed new instruments are research proposals. No present tax-law or treaty status is asserted. Legal adoption, entry-into-force, and applicability dates are not applicable.

- After the first model run: added foreign producer surplus and the global-loss identity. This reports the foreign loss that can support a domestic gain. Removed an unused external-grant input; no grant experiment is claimed.
