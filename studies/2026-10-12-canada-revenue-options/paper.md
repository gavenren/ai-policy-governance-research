# Can Canada Fund a Living After Wages?
## Options for C$1.10 trillion in new annual revenue

**Plain-English policy analysis | 12 October 2026 | Canadian dollars**  
**Status:** AI-assisted analysis with separate AI checks. The tax packages below are constructed cases. They are not official costings, forecasts, or endorsed tax rates. No human peer review has occurred.

## The main finding

Canada could fund a large income guarantee only if it can collect a large share of income that no longer goes to workers. That income might take the form of company profits, investment returns, or other asset income. Taxes on consumption could provide a second source, with rebates to protect people on low incomes. Public ownership could add income after its purchase and financing costs are paid.

There is no verified current revenue package here that supplies C$1.10 trillion a year. Wealth, land, and resource taxes can contribute, but the cited Canadian amounts are much smaller. Borrowing can cover a temporary gap. It does not create a permanent revenue source.

Two constructed packages cover the target when the economy is about twice the starting size, taxable capital income is 40% of GDP, and 90% of the calculated tax is collected before retained GST credits and rebates. One uses a 45% combined federal charge on capital income and a 10% federal GST. Another uses 40% and 15%, with a larger rebate. These are demanding assumptions. Neither package covers the target at the starting GDP under the same base assumptions. Neither proves that the money is collectible or that people retain an adequate living standard.

The practical task is to build a joint federal and provincial tax plan, measure the future bases, and protect the value of the payment. Government should not turn a calculation that balances into a claim that the policy is ready.

## 1. What the C$1.10 trillion target means

The [Canada policy application](../2026-10-12-canada-policy-application/paper.md) tested an **additional, tax-free C$24,000 for each of 34.16 million adults**. Existing benefits remain. The payment is excluded from other benefit income tests. This is a much larger promise than bringing each person's total income up to C$24,000.

Its central case has C$867.58 billion in new annual spending. It also assumes a loss of 80% of federal personal income tax and all Employment Insurance premiums, worth C$232.54 billion. Together, these create a need for **C$1,100.12 billion in new annual revenue**. The assumption about personal tax is not an observed wage-tax share. The account is a stress test.

| Account carried forward | C$ billion a year |
| --- | ---: |
| Existing revenue left after the assumed losses | 295.76 |
| Federal expense, including the new payment and stated additions | 1,467.68 |
| Starting deficit that the target permits | 71.80 |
| **Additional revenue needed to keep that deficit unchanged** | **1,100.12** |
| Additional revenue needed to balance the budget | 1,171.92 |

The target already includes an assumed expansion of child support, C$15 billion in added services, and benefit administration. It does not include provincial tax losses, all possible changes in benefit claims, or interest on extra borrowing. Those costs do not disappear because a federal table balances.

The starting GDP is C$3,392 billion, from the PBO's June 2026 forecast. The target is about 32.4% of that GDP **in additional revenue**. Future cases retain the earlier paper's fixed 2026-price benchmark. Population, the benefit promise, and baseline service costs stay fixed. No date is assigned to the end of wages or to a larger economy. [PBO, June 2026 outlook](https://www.pbo-dpb.ca/en/publications/RP-2627-002-S--economic-fiscal-outlook-june-2026--perspectives-economiques-financieres-juin-2026)

## 2. Compare the funding options

| Option | Useful role | Main limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Company profits and owner income** | Main candidate if AI shifts income from wages to owners. Use company tax, taxes on dividends and gains, and charges on excess profits together. | Income can move abroad, be deferred, or disappear through competition. Company and owner taxes share a base. |
| **GST or another broad consumption tax** | Reaches spending even where profits are hard to locate. Can apply to taxable imports. | Reduces buying power unless payment design and rebates offset the burden. Exemptions reduce the base. |
| **Annual wealth tax** | Collects from large asset holdings and can address concentrated ownership. | Wealth is a stock. Values, avoidance, cash needs, and other capital taxes change the yield. |
| **Land and resource income** | Can collect returns tied to a location or a public resource. | Existing receipts fund other governments. Land, buildings, resource profits, and company taxes overlap. |
| **Public ownership** | Gives the public a direct claim on future returns. | Assets must be acquired or built. Debt, losses, fees, and reinvestment reduce cash available. |
| **Borrowing, reserves, or money creation** | Can help manage a temporary shock or payment delay. | They do not establish a durable claim on enough goods and services. Debt adds interest; spending can exceed real supply. |

### Company and owner taxes: the central source to test

The aim should be to collect from returns across the economy, rather than depend on a narrow definition of an AI company. A tax on excess profit can target returns above a stated normal return. Broader capital-income taxes can reach returns outside that category. Neither base is known in advance. The IMF supports stronger capital-income taxation and consideration of excess-profit taxes as responses to AI-related inequality; it does not establish the rates or yields in this paper. [IMF, fiscal policy and generative AI, 2024](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/Staff-Discussion-Notes/Issues/2024/06/11/Broadening-the-Gains-from-Generative-AI-The-Role-of-Fiscal-Policies-549639)

Company profit, dividends, and capital gains are not three independent pools of money. Canada already uses dividend tax credits to account for presumed company tax. For example, a C$20 company tax on C$100 leaves C$80. A 25% owner tax on that remainder adds C$20, for C$40 in total. Adding the two rates as if they both applied to C$100 would give the wrong result. [Finance Canada, dividend tax treatment](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/services/publications/federal-tax-expenditures/2026/part-5.html)

The model below therefore uses one combined federal charge on one capital-income base. It does not add a separate wealth-tax estimate or a second dividend tax on top. Provincial taxes still matter. The federal rates shown are not the total Canadian burden.

Canada also cannot claim all global AI profit. Some treaties limit Canadian taxation of a foreign firm's business profits where it has no Canadian permanent establishment. Exact treaty terms matter. International agreement, enforcement, and the allocation of profits are part of the funding plan, not details to solve after spending is promised. [CRA, non-resident business income](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax-audit-manual-domestic-compliance-programs-branch-dcpb-15.html)

### Consumption tax: broad reach, with a real cost to recipients

A consumption tax can collect from spending funded by asset income. Canada already requires certain foreign digital suppliers or platforms to collect GST/HST on taxable sales to Canadian consumers. This taxes Canadian consumption; it does not transfer all foreign supplier profit to Canada. [CRA, cross-border digital supplies](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/charge-collect/cross-border.html)

For scale, household consumption totalled C$1,761.235 billion in 2025. That is not a GST base. It includes sales taxes, imputed rent, and purchases with different tax treatment. Applying a rate to that whole number would overstate a usable base. Current GST receipts are also net of credits and rebates. [Statistics Canada, household consumption](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610012401); [CRA, administered financial statements, note 11](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-financial-statements/cra-fs-administered-activities.html)

Taxing spending out of a public payment returns some cash to government. It also leaves less for goods. The new rebates below are explicit costs, but their adequacy is untested. If the purpose is to preserve a fixed living standard, the payment or rebate may need to rise further. A package that meets the cash target can still fail that purpose.

### Wealth, land, and resources: useful supplements

PBO's 19 April 2025 costing of a proposed annual wealth tax estimated C$23.042 billion in 2026-27, about **2.1% of this target**. The proposal used rates of 1%, 2%, and 3% on wealth above specified high thresholds. It included assumptions about hidden wealth and collection costs. This is a dated proposal estimate, not an enacted tax or an estimate for an economy without wages. Its yield cannot be scaled directly with the rate or added unchanged to a new capital-tax system. [PBO, wealth-tax proposal](https://www.pbo-dpb.ca/en/epc-estimates--estimations-cpe/45/EL-45-1023246-P)

Household net worth was about C$19.1 trillion in the second quarter of 2026. The annual target equals about **5.8% of that entire stock**. This comparison includes holdings that a practical tax might exempt and assets without ready cash. Residential property is already part of wealth; it cannot be added again as an independent asset pool. The ratio is a scale check, not a revenue estimate. [Statistics Canada, national balance sheet](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260911/dq260911a-eng.htm)

Land taxes need separate land valuations, rules for cash-poor owners, and agreements on which government keeps the receipts. Local property taxes already raised C$84.417 billion in 2024 for existing public duties. Taking those receipts for a federal payment would leave another budget gap. [Finance Canada, Fiscal Reference Tables, Table 42](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/services/publications/fiscal-reference-tables/2025/part-7.html)

Resource royalties and charges on scarce sites, power access, or other public concessions could help. Their yield depends on prices, costs, rights, and the share already collected. A resource company's profit can also be in the company-tax base. No new royalty yield is counted here. Provinces have specific powers over resources, and Indigenous rights and agreements require separate treatment. [Constitution Act, section 92A](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/const/section-92A.html)

### Public ownership: a long-term claim with an initial cost

At an assumed 3% annual withdrawal after costs and inflation, a fund with no debt would need **C$36.67 trillion in assets** to pay C$1.10 trillion each year. At 2%, it needs C$55.01 trillion; at 4%, C$27.50 trillion. None of these rates is guaranteed.

Buying shares with taxes uses money that cannot also pay current benefits. Buying them with debt adds interest and repayment duties. Taking shares through a levy is a tax paid in assets; it is not immediate cash. Selling assets can fund a transition but reduces future income. A government must also avoid adding company tax and a public dividend as if each had a claim on the same pre-tax profit. Fund income must be measured after tax and financing costs.

CPP and QPP assets already support pension claims. They are excluded. Moving an asset while leaving the pension obligation in place creates no net public wealth. [Canada Pension Plan Investment Board Act, section 5](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/section-5.html); [Retraite Québec, QPP](https://www.retraitequebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/programs/quebec-pension-plan)

## 3. A common account for three tax packages

The following cases ask what tax bases and rates could meet the target. They are not recommendations to enact these rates today. Each assumption is held fixed so that the cases can be compared.

| Assumption | Value and meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Economic size | 1, 1.5, 2, or 3 times the C$3,392bn benchmark, at fixed prices. These are scenarios, not growth forecasts. |
| Capital-income base | 40% of GDP. Assumed taxable net income after depreciation, exclusions, and cross-border allocation, before federal and provincial income taxes. This is not a measured future share. |
| Consumption base | 60% of GDP in taxable final purchases, excluding consumption taxes. This is not all household spending. |
| Collection | 90% of the calculated charge for each tax. This is a selected allowance for shortfalls, before the retained GST relief below. It is not a measured collection forecast. |
| Retained GST credits and rebates | C$20bn a year, deducted from reformed GST receipts before replacing the old net GST line. This is a selected allowance, not an estimate of current or future legal obligations. |
| New rebates | C$100bn, C$150bn, or C$200bn each year, depending on the package. These are additional to existing credits and the original payment. |
| New tax administration | C$10bn a year, additional to benefit administration already in the target. It is a planning allowance. |
| Other receipts and spending | Retained baseline receipts outside the replaced taxes stay fixed. The original real benefit and service budget stays fixed. |

The collection factor is not a full model of avoidance, investment, or prices. The capital and consumption bases must eventually be estimated together. This model does not prove that households can make the assumed purchases after all taxes.

### Subtract the taxes being replaced

The original account retains C$295.76 billion in revenue. This model groups C$165.16 billion as existing capital-related receipts: C$101.2 billion in company tax, C$49.66 billion in retained personal tax, and C$14.3 billion in non-resident tax. It also replaces C$52.8 billion of existing GST. This grouping is a stated modelling choice; the retained personal-tax line is not a measured capital-income tax base.

The reform must replace those receipts before it produces money for the new target. C$77.8 billion of other retained revenue stays outside the reform account. Thus:

**Net new resources = reformed capital receipts + GST receipts before relief - retained GST relief - C$217.96bn of replaced receipts - extra rebates - new tax administration.**

The C$217.96 billion is subtracted once. The C$20 billion allowance pays retained GST credits and rebates under the reformed system. The C$100-200 billion new rebate is separate. Since the old C$52.8 billion GST line is already net of its credits, replacing it with new receipts after relief avoids counting old receipts twice. The actual retained relief cost needs official estimation; if it is higher, net funding is lower.

### Results with an economy twice the starting size

All rates below are **total federal rates in the new case**, not increases added to old rates. The capital rate is an assumed average combined federal charge on company and owner income after tax credits, before collection losses. It is not a statutory rate for each taxpayer. GST is stated as a rate on the price before tax. Provincial rates are not included.

| Annual account, C$bn unless a rate | More from capital | Mixed package | More from consumption |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Combined federal capital rate | 45% | 40% | 30% |
| Federal GST rate | 10% | 15% | 20% |
| Capital receipts, before subtracting old receipts | 1,099.01 | 976.90 | 732.67 |
| GST receipts, before retained relief and the extra rebate | 366.34 | 549.50 | 732.67 |
| Retained GST credits and rebates | -20.00 | -20.00 | -20.00 |
| Replaced receipts | -217.96 | -217.96 | -217.96 |
| Extra rebate | -100.00 | -150.00 | -200.00 |
| Extra tax administration | -10.00 | -10.00 | -10.00 |
| **Net new resources** | **1,117.38** | **1,128.44** | **1,017.38** |

For example, the mixed case calculates capital receipts as C$6,784bn × 40% base × 40% rate × 90% collection. Its GST receipts before relief are C$6,784bn × 60% base × 15% rate × 90% collection. It then deducts the C$20bn retained-relief allowance. This explains the result; it does not prove that these bases will exist under these rates.

The first two packages meet the target under the stated assumptions. They still leave federal deficits of about C$54.54 billion and C$43.48 billion, because the target itself allows a deficit. Their surpluses over the target are not proof of resilience. Provincial costs remain outside the calculation.

## 4. How easily the result fails

| Net new resources, C$bn a year | Starting GDP | 1.5 times GDP | 2 times GDP | 3 times GDP |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| More from capital | 384.71 | 751.05 | 1,117.38 | 1,850.06 |
| Mixed package | 365.24 | 746.84 | 1,128.44 | 1,891.64 |
| More from consumption | 284.71 | 651.05 | 1,017.38 | 1,750.06 |

With every other assumption fixed, the packages reach the target at about **1.98, 1.96, and 2.11 times** the starting GDP, respectively. These are thresholds in this model, not minimum economic sizes that apply to every possible tax design. Different bases, public spending, or rates change them.

A larger AI capability does not automatically produce a larger Canadian tax base. Low prices can spread benefits while reducing taxable profit. Foreign ownership can move returns abroad. A rise in money prices alone also raises living costs; it does not meet a real-income promise.

The mixed package shows the risk clearly. Change one assumption at a time from its twice-GDP case:

| Change | Net new resources, C$bn | Result against the target |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| No change | 1,128.44 | C$28.32bn above |
| Capital base falls from 40% to 25% of GDP | 762.10 | C$338.02bn short |
| Collection falls from 90% to 70% for both taxes | 789.24 | C$310.88bn short |
| Taxable consumption falls from 60% to 45% of GDP | 991.06 | C$109.06bn short |
| Extra rebate rises by 50%, to C$225bn | 1,053.44 | C$46.68bn short |
| Retained GST credits and rebates rise to C$40bn | 1,108.44 | C$8.32bn above |

These are selected stress cases, not confidence bounds. Combined shocks could be worse. They also show why a rebate cannot be treated as an afterthought.

Relying on only one tax creates more pressure. With the assumed capital base and collection rate, capital taxes alone would need a combined federal rate of about **104.4% at the starting GDP**, so the required rate exceeds 100% before the collection reduction. At twice GDP the rate would be about **52.2%**, before provincial taxes. A GST-only case with a C$200 billion extra rebate would need about **75.5% at the starting GDP** or **37.8% at twice GDP**. These are warning calculations, not workable proposals. Prices and tax bases would respond.

## 5. Sources that do not close the account by themselves

**Repealing tax breaks.** Some reforms can broaden the base. But Finance Canada warns that its tax-expenditure estimates are static and interact. Their published values cannot simply be added as the revenue from repeal. Better enforcement also needs a net estimate after staff, systems, legal limits, and taxpayer response. Neither is booked as a separate windfall here. [Finance Canada, interpretation of tax-expenditure estimates](https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/services/publications/federal-tax-expenditures/2026/part-1.html)

**International tax agreements.** They can limit profit shifting and allocate taxing rights. The current global minimum-tax system is not a Canadian claim on all world profits. Existing measures are already in the baseline. Count only an additional, enforceable Canadian share. [CRA, global minimum tax](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/taxes/excise-taxes-duties-and-levies/global-minimum-tax/about.html)

**Permanent borrowing.** If Canada borrows the full C$1.10 trillion gap each year at a fixed 3% real rate, and also borrows to pay interest on opening debt, new debt reaches about C$12.61 trillion after ten years. Interest in year ten alone is about C$335.29 billion. This illustration excludes existing debt and assumes the rate stays fixed. It is not a debt forecast. A reserve changes payment timing, not this annual funding need.

**Money creation.** Creating money can change how spending is financed. It cannot ensure that homes, care, energy, and imported goods are available. If nominal spending exceeds supply, prices or exchange rates can change and reduce the value of the payment. This paper assigns no recurring yield to money creation and models no monetary regime.

**Changing the payment promise.** Testing income, recovering payments from wealthy households, or replacing existing benefits could reduce cost. Those choices need distributional and legal checks. They change the additional, tax-free payment tested here. They should be shown as a new policy case rather than called revenue for an unchanged promise.

## 6. What governments should do

**First, define the real promise and the whole public account.** Federal, provincial, territorial, local, and Indigenous partners should agree on duties and revenue shares. Set the living standard, eligibility, benefit interactions, and the treatment of housing and care. Include provincial losses and pension funding. Ottawa cannot spend receipts that another government still needs for existing services.

**Second, measure and cost the tax bases jointly.** Finance Canada, CRA, and PBO should publish a common model of company income, owner income, consumption, and wealth. It should include foreign ownership, depreciation, losses, investment, avoidance, and household effects. Compare direct taxation of capital first, then the amount of consumption tax and rebate needed. Report gross receipts, replaced receipts, and net new money separately.

**Third, build collection and payment capacity before a large expansion.** Improve ownership and asset information, cross-border reporting, tax administration, appeals, and non-digital payment access. Test essential services and construction plans in parallel. Neither a company valuation nor a tax assessment guarantees cash on the payment date.

**Fourth, fund immediate hardship while long-term reform proceeds.** Use an explicit emergency budget when needed. Temporary borrowing may be part of that budget, with stated costs. Do not wait for proof that AI caused each income loss. Do not use an assumed future asset return as current revenue.

**Finally, expand the permanent promise only with an updated account.** Publish at least annual tests of collected revenue, payment value, essential supply, and adverse cases. New rebates, provincial support, and financing costs must enter the same account. Public ownership can be added after its acquisition and net cash returns are shown. Wealth and land taxes can be tested as supplements after their interactions are counted.

A revenue system centred on capital income, with a consumption tax and adequate rebates, is the main route worth costing. This analysis establishes the scale and the conditions of that route. It does not establish that Canada can collect C$1.10 trillion each year under today's conditions, or under every economy in which AI outcompetes workers.
