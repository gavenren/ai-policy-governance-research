**Figure. Equal budgets, different outcomes.** Three payment rules use the same aggregate gross monthly budget: $2,000 times the survey-weighted number of included SCF families. The rules pay equally per family, equally per adult aged 18 or above, or in proportion to the square root of family size. The common floor is $1,000 times the square root of family size. The original wage-loss experiment and fixed debt payments are retained.

Panel a shows the survey-weighted percentage of all included families whose resources fall below the floor after debt payments. Panel b weights the same family result by the number of family members: it shows the percentage of people living in such families, not individual deprivation or consumption. Panel c shows the survey-weighted mean monthly dollar shortfall per family. Its denominator includes families with zero shortfall. “Family” means the SCF primary economic unit, which need not include everyone at the same address. The primary sample contains 4,358 families per implicate; two families with top-coded size are excluded.

Points and labels are the published estimates. Bars are the saved normal-approximation 95% intervals. These combine sampling and imputation variance from five implicates and 999 first-implicate replicates, with payment coefficients refitted in every implicate and replicate. Panel a complements the published pass share and reverses its saved interval endpoints. No model or uncertainty estimate was recalculated. Intervals describe separate levels; overlap does not test the paired differences between rules. The horizontal scales differ across panels. The comparison is conditional on the stated experiment and does not establish a welfare ranking or fiscal feasibility.
