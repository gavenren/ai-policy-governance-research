## Decision and research question

A short comment is feasible. A new survey estimate is not yet justified. The useful comparison concerns an actual proposed clause: section 3(e) of H.R. 5830, the Guaranteed Income Pilot Program Act of 2025. The clause excludes a payment from income when eligibility for federally funded programs is determined. The 2026 Medicaid rule uses MAGI household income both for financial eligibility and for a lower-income route to satisfy its community-engagement condition. Does a broadly written disregard also prevent the new payment from helping to meet that lower test? The study can set out the two readings and an explicit federal drafting option.

The contribution is limited to this textual interaction. Benefit cliffs, income disregards, and benefit protection are established topics. Baker and coauthors describe protection methods in the Stockton pilot. Varcie and coauthors study routes to Medicaid exclusions and compliance under the new rule. NYC's official comment already disputes the exclusion of some unearned income from the lower test. The bounded search did not identify a direct analysis of H.R. 5830 section 3(e) together with section 435.552(f). This is not proof of worldwide novelty.

## The comparison must keep its baseline

A payment disregarded for both tests leaves those two income inputs at their no-payment values when all other income and conditions are held fixed. It does not remove a route that already existed. It can forgo an additional route that otherwise-counted cash could open. Counted cash can also cross the upper financial limit. A proposed split treatment would exclude the payment for that upper limit and expressly credit it for the lower test. This is a new federal design option, not a state software setting.

The strongest contrary reading is that the bill protects the position before a payment. It need not promise every additional advantage of counted income. Its protective purpose might also support a distinction between the two inquiries. Its broad eligibility wording and the rule's shared income method make express clarification useful, but do not establish a binding interpretation. The paper must not say that the bill causes current coverage loss or has a proven defect.

The parent protocol adds a no-payment treatment and ten fixed cases. The cases show accounting only. The source lead did not execute case code, fit survey data, or draft the central paper. Earlier informal examples discussed within the team are exploratory. They are not prospective discoveries.

## Scope and legal status

H.R. 5830 is an introduced bill in the supplied official record. Its last recorded action is referral to Ways and Means on October 24, 2025. The record was updated on November 21, 2025 and retrieved on October 12, 2026. That is fresh access to the supplied record, not a guarantee that every later event is represented. The bill proposes a three-year pilot, 20,000 selected people and payments to 10,000, with age and taxpayer conditions. Payments refer to two-bedroom fair-market rent or a substantially similar amount. It is not a universal AI dividend. Section 2 does mention automation. In section 3(e), the 12-month limit concerns resources, not income.

The June 3, 2026 rule is an interim final rule, effective July 31, 2026. The statutory implementation date is generally January 1, 2027; specified good-faith relief can run no later than December 31, 2028. Earlier state implementation is possible. The June 29 correction replaces verification and noncompliance text in sections 435.557 and 435.558. The inspected current eCFR title 42 displayed an October 8, 2026 currency date. Its text is an authoritative but unofficial compilation.

The statutory base is Public Law 119-21 section 71119, which adds section 1902(xx). Section 1902(e)(14) supplies the general MAGI method and limits state disregards, subject to its exceptions. The SSA compilation was used for that older provision only; the enacted Public Law supplies the new subsection. The rule expressly rejects an earned-income-only reading. It uses the person's MAGI household for the month under review, including countable unearned income. A survey household is not automatically that legal unit. Seasonal-worker averaging and state options for predictable income require separate treatment.

Community service, qualifying work programs, study and combinations can provide other routes. Specified exclusions and optional hardship provisions also matter. Total loss of competitive wages does not prove that all adults fail those routes. High unemployment relief is a state option with conditions. Loss of labor-force participation is not itself the unemployment-rate trigger. The statute bars a section 1115 waiver of subsection (xx); that does not turn every existing state option into a prohibition. A federal split rule must be drafted expressly and its cost assessed.

The statutory process includes notice, a chance to respond, assessment of other eligibility bases and hearing rights. A simple failed income check is not an observed coverage termination. The July 29 order in Massachusetts v. Oz denies preliminary relief without prejudice and expressly reserves the merits. An October 8 notice sets a later hearing. This check used public copies of court records on a university site. It was not an exhaustive docket search and does not show that all possible legal challenges have been resolved.

## Data gate and closest work

Varcie et al. use the 2024 ACS as their main data. CPS data support a medical-frailty imputation. Appendix A2 explains its annual-income and work-hours proxies and their imperfect match to monthly MAGI. The public supplement contains documents and figures, not analysis code. The wording alone does not establish a computational error. We do not correct their estimates or use their outcome counts as predictions for an AI economy.

The SIPP guide is a useful negative check. Some annual income is divided equally across the reference year's months. The resulting monthly variable does not necessarily observe the actual timing of receipts. Its family and household recodes also do not directly determine all legal MAGI units. A credible population estimate would need a separate identification plan for income timing, legal unit membership, program category, other routes, verification and uncertain imputation. No such plan has passed this screen.
