## S3: Relational Incentive Contracts

Jonathan Levin. 2003-06. [American Economic Review 93(3), 835-857](https://web.stanford.edu/~jdlevin/Papers/RIC.pdf).

Location: Section III and Theorem 3; PDF pages 7-8, printed pages 841-842.

> there are bounds on the compensation

Interpretation: Dynamic enforcement limits promised transfers relative to the outside option; established theory.

## S5: Verification for International AI Governance

Ben Harack; Robert F. Trager; Anka Reuel; David Manheim; Miles Brundage; and coauthors listed on PDF page 2. 2025-07. [Report; exact date in capture if stated](https://aigi.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Verification_for_International_AI_Governance.pdf).

## S6: Financial Transfers, Compliance Technology and Climate Cooperation

Suzi Kerr; Steffen Lippert; Edmund Lou. 2018-02-16. [Preliminary and incomplete working paper, dated February 16, 2018](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/saet/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/gravity_forms/1-0eb242c25464f1b25dc5136cf190c7a3/2018/02/FinTransClimate_006c.pdf).

Location: Introduction pages 1-4; Sections 3.1-3.4 and Propositions 3-5.

> We consider different timings of these payments and different degrees of commitment.

Interpretation: Transfer timing and credible third-party commitment in climate cooperation are existing contributions.

## S8: Safe Transformative AI via a Windfall Clause

Paolo Bova, Jonas Emanuel Müller, and Benjamin Harack. 2021-08-28. [arXiv:2108.09404v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09404v2). Location: Section 2.2.2.

> The second challenge is building the credibility of the Windfall Clause

Interpretation: The study models firm participation and safety incentives. Its discussion distinguishes commitment from changes in incentives as firms learn their relative capabilities. It is not evidence that country-to-country payments are enforceable.
