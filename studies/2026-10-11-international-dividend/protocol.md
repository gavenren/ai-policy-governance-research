# S009 protocol and design record

State: Working notes. Recorded at 2026-10-11T21:25:34Z, after source search and conceptual derivation but before the offline model was implemented. This is an exploratory theory study, not a preregistration.

## Question and contribution

When can a recurring international AI transfer protect a fixed real income floor if the paying country can withhold payment after it receives the partner's cooperation? Study a specified stationary, state-contingent agreement, with a credible noncooperation continuation, observable Markov states, a domestic spending minimum, and an optional seized-on-default deposit. Derive an exact feasibility test and counterexamples to using average surplus or total AI wealth as measures of payment security.

The test specializes established relational-contract logic. Dennis et al. (2025) already propose financial sharing and discuss credibility. Levin (2003) already gives dynamic enforcement constraints. Kerr, Lippert, and Lou (2018) already compare international transfer timing and credible third parties. The incremental contribution is the joint floor, Markov persistence, domestic-budget, and deposit-cost audit in a conditional post-labor setting. No general novelty claim is made.

## Scope and units

One provider government and one recipient government (which could represent a coalition) interact indefinitely. Household support has no reliable wage component. Country income, real needs, and cooperation rents are fixed by an external state; no claim is made to forecast those states. Country-level fiscal payoffs and recipient household resources are separate. There is no legal instrument comparison. All treaty and escrow rules are model proposals, not descriptions of current law.

## Planned analysis

Use a small standard-library model with transparent linear algebra. The main experiment holds the stationary distribution of two states fixed while changing their persistence. Test a fixed floor in every state; report the maximum floor within this agreement class. Compare no deposit with a bounded deposit. Separately vary total provider resources and the surplus actually lost on exit. Include zero cooperation surplus, a binding domestic spending rule, high and low carrying costs, and a positive case. Carrying cost is a fiscal opportunity or funding cost and can contain real administration expense; it is not automatically a global resource loss.

A deterministic grid will vary discounting, symmetric persistence, adverse-state cooperation gain, domestic spending headroom, deposit capacity, and deposit carrying cost. Inputs will be published before running the main grid. Report all cases, including infeasibility at a zero floor, and no statistical significance or population-frequency claims. No probabilities of actual AI outcomes will be estimated.

## Known restrictions

Perfect public monitoring, no private information, risk-neutral government payoff, externally fixed transitions, no renegotiation-proofness, no sovereign borrowing or domestic redistribution game, and no endogenous foreign cooperation choice beyond a binary action. The primary theorem is only for the stated stationary payment schedule and punishment. It is not a universal impossibility result for every treaty.
