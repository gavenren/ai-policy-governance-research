# Policy and evidence review

**Date:** 12 October 2026  
**Reviewer:** Separate Codex AI review, Canada delivery task  
**Scope:** Canadian institutions, order of action, urgent support, source support, and plain English. This is not human peer review, legal advice, or an official costing. The reviewer also supplied the delivery source notes and is not an independent external reviewer.

## Finding

The paper gives a clear, conditional Canadian application. It separates a limited transition budget from the much larger cost of a universal additional payment. It does not claim that the first tax package funds the severe case. It states that the new measures require approval and that current legal duties do not already cover the proposal.

One clarification is required before publication. No other major policy or institutional error was found in the reviewed draft.

## Required clarification

**State the tax and benefit treatment of the limited income top-up.** Section 4 defines the severe-case payment as tax-free and excluded from other benefit tests. Section 2 says existing benefits continue and claims no savings, but does not state whether the smaller top-up has the same treatment. Add a short sentence. For the budget as written, the clean assumption is that the top-up is tax-free and does not reduce existing benefits. State that these protections require the relevant laws and agreements. An alternative design is possible, but the associated tax recovery and benefit losses need their own account.

This clarification matters because the stated $12 billion is a gross payment allowance. Without a defined tax and benefit treatment, readers cannot tell whether it is also the intended gain before new GST costs. It does not replace the required household analysis.

## Checks that passed

- The paper uses CRA's announced March 2027 pre-filled-return service as a delivery starting point. It correctly says users still submit returns and the service is not universal enrolment.
- The paper preserves Canada's existing publicly insured hospital and physician care. It does not import an employer-insurance assumption from the United States.
- The role of provinces and territories in services and benefit interactions is clear. The paper correctly says the largely unconditional Canada Social Transfer does not already impose the proposed protection rules.
- The paper calls for direct Indigenous participation. This appears as a proposed design requirement. It does not claim to give a complete legal account of Indigenous rights or service arrangements.
- CPP and QPP assets are excluded from new general-programme finance. The official sources support their existing pension purposes.
- The housing scale uses the Fall 2026 CMHC estimate. The paper does not claim that its $5 billion grant line can deliver the whole estimated supply need.
- Preparation, tax work and construction overlap. Emergency support can start before the scheduled 2028–29 payment launch. The text expressly identifies extra emergency appropriations as costs outside the scheduled budget.
- The recipient count is a planning assumption, not a cap on an entitlement. Expansion would require additional funding and could require larger delivery and service allowances.
- The GST rebate is additional and must reach low-income people beyond the assumed top-up recipients. The paper does not claim its adequacy has been established.
- The paper distinguishes annual spending, one-time setup, a cash reserve, and borrowing. It shows that the reserve cannot solve a permanent funding gap.
- The severe case identifies important omitted costs and states that the federal account is not an all-government affordability estimate.
- Source language and proposed actions remain distinguishable. Dates describe a proposed plan, not laws already passed or a forecast of AI progress.

## Optional improvements

The phrase “current account” in section 3 could become “annual revenue and spending account” to avoid confusion with the balance-of-payments term. The existing explanation makes the intended meaning clear, so this is a style point.

The paper could explicitly say that early October 2026–March 2027 planning uses existing departmental budgets, if that is the author's assumption. Any new spending in that interval still needs to enter the cost table. This is a useful boundary note rather than evidence of an omitted quantified cost.

## Limits of this check

This review did not validate population extraction, PBO fiscal inputs, tax yield, household outcomes, or every calculation. Those require the separate source and arithmetic checks. Several linked support files were still being prepared at the time of this review. Their presence and links need a publication check. No evidence here proves that the proposed taxes, service targets, or payment routes will work in practice.

## Resolution check

The revised paper was checked on 12 October 2026. Section 2 now states that the limited income support is tax-free and does not reduce existing benefits. It also states that legislation and agreements are required. The input file records this assumption, and the analysis checks it. The required clarification is resolved.

The paper also replaces the ambiguous “current account” wording. The input file and analysis record the assumption that planning before April 2027 uses existing departmental budgets. These changes clarify scope and do not require a change in the stated payment arithmetic. Numerical validation remains the task of the separate fiscal review.

**Final policy review result:** No unresolved required correction within this review's scope. Publication still requires the stated source, arithmetic and link checks. This remains an AI review, not external human review or government approval.
